Who is your project for? Who is it about? 
These two questions allow you to identify the people most directly impacted by your DHH project. 

## ACTIVITY
Make a list of all of the individuals or groups of people whose stories are contained within the data for your project. This could include people who are no longer alive, as well as their living friends, family members, and descendants; it could include people who share a similar diagnosis, practitioners, advocates, stockholders, etc.
You know your project best. Make a list of everyone who could possibly feel like they have a stake in what you are doing. Some terms that could be used to describe these people may include “audience” and “community.” 
After you have listed everyone who immediately comes to mind, see if you can think of any more by answering these questions:  
1. Whose stories are included in the archive your project is based on? Did they have a choice about their stories becoming part of your archive?
2. Who created the data or archive you are working with? 
3. Is your project about or for your community? Your family? Your institution?
4. Who else is impacted by or connected to your work? Consider: 
- Current residents/members/people living with a diagnosis?
- Those who are no longer present?
- Users of a space?
- Ethnic groups? Religious groups?
- Who else is impacted by your project?

Look over your list: do any of the people or groups on the list think of themselves as being a “community”? Or as being in community with others on the list? Are there active or latent conflicts between people on your list? All of these are important to be aware of in structuring an ethical approach to your project. 

How can you engage ethically with those who are impacted by your project? Each of these groups will have different needs, as well as different ways in which they are privileged and marginalized by society. 

## ACTIVITY

Revisit your list, and select one group to work with. You may want to think about who the project is for–as in, who do you hope that this project will help, directly or indirectly? 

Once you have selected a group, identify which of the privileged and marginalized identities that the members of this mostly group hold, using this worksheet.   

Looking over the worksheet, what do you notice about this group that you might not have thought about before?

Do they have a choice about engaging with the archive or with your project?
