When we think about the items we use to conduct our research, we often see them as items or objects, and not as data, especially in more traditional humanities disciplines. In order to conduct digital humanities research, you need to pull data from somewhere, and even though we may not think about our research items in this way, they contain data that can help us carry out a digital project. 

Let’s stop and think about what data our objects may contain. It is possible that an object can contain multiple types of data. 

For this section of the activity, answer the following questions about 1-2 objects of your choosing. 

1. Why is this object important to your project?
2. Have you researched this object before? If so, what research methods have you applied to this object in the past?
3. How has this object helped answer these research questions? How do you imagine it would help answer these questions?
4. Does this object contain metadata (Author, Date, Publisher, Page Number, Medium, Geographic Location)? If so, what does it contain? 
5. What can you pull from this object? (Text, images, geographic locations, names. . .)
6. What do you need to do to make this object readable for your digital method?
7. What new research questions come to mind as you look at your object from a digital methods lens?
