Now that we have a general understanding of how to approach digital humanities research, we can begin using low-barrier digital tools. In the following section, we will use digital tools that do not require much training. When we first incorporate digital approaches into our research, we can start with low-barrier tools to help us develop our technical skills and build our confidence in these new approaches. 

## Step 1: Digital mapping 
One tool that does not have a steep learning curve for mapping is Google My Maps. This platform is similar to Google Maps that you may use to look for directions. It has the same look and feel as Google Maps, but Google My Maps allows you to create and customize maps. This means we can create a map that fits our research needs. If you want to learn more about the platform, follow this link. 

For this activity, we will use Google My Maps to create a digital geographic representation of the primary source we worked with previously: The oral history of Lucille Cowley Nixon. If you need to refresh your memory on the primary source, please re-read it before we begin. Some of the unique features of Google My Maps include: adding points, lines, and polygons (shapes) to a map. You can also add layers that you can toggle on and off depending on how you want to visualize your data. 
Set a 15-minute timer and create a digital map of the locations Lucille mentions in her oral history. 
For instructions on how to use Google My Maps visit this link. 
To begin your map, go to Google My Maps. You may need to log in to a Gmail account. Feel free to use any of the tools the platform offers, such as points, lines, layers, and polygons.

## Step 2: Reflection

Let’s focus on how the process of using a digital tool, like Google My Maps, compares to creating a hand-drawn map, like in the previous section. 

- How did you feel using Google My Maps? 
- Were there aspects that Google My Maps made easier? If so, what were they and how? 
- What challenges did you have in using Google My Maps? 
- How does your digital map compare to your hand-drawn map? 
- How did creating this digital visualization alter your initial interpretation of the primary source?
