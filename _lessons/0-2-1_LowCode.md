For decades, scholars have been defining the discipline of the digital humanities. Among the common themes in its definition are: digital tools, research, and humanities. [Miriam Posner](https://seis.ucla.edu/faculty/miriam-posner/) defines the digital humanities as the use of digital tools to explore humanities questions. The field has expanded since its initial inception in the 1940s with textual analysis. Nowadays, the digital humanities encompasses a wide array of subfields, like network analysis, mapping, digital exhibits, and data visualization. In this tutorial, we provide a brief introduction to the digital humanities to ground the work we will do. If you would like a more extensive overview of the digital humanities, [please refer to this tutorial]({{ base.url }}/lessons/intro/whatisDH/1).  

You can also see other digital humanities definitions [here](https://whatisdigitalhumanities.com/). When you refresh the page, you will see a new definition of digital humanities. 

In this tutorial, we will discuss how you can approach the application of digital methods to your research. We will begin by discussing how we typically start a new research project, then engage in hands-on activities to consider digital methods from a conceptual standpoint. We will then discuss your research projects and walk you through how you can begin applying digital methods to them. The final section is an overview of the value that the digital humanities brings to research. 

## Back to Basics

Before we begin discussing how we can incorporate digital methods into our research, let’s begin by thinking about how we generally approach research. For this activity, we will think about the steps we typically take when we begin a new research project, and we will engage with a primary source to help us apply some of those initial steps.
