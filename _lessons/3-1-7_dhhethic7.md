**This current lesson is an outline. There are elements that still need to be completed/fleshed out.**

At the conclusion of your project, reflect back on your ethical practices, alongside a broader reflection/evaluation of the project. What surprised you? What aspects of ethical practices did you find yourself resisting? Which exercises supported the ethical completion of your project? How could you have handled things differently? 

The point of this process of reflection is not self-flagellation, and as with so much of your ethical practices, will neither be required nor of interest to funders or supervisors. You can’t undo the past, but you can apply the lessons learned to the future, to the next project you work on that engages with community, in one way or another. 

## Activity: An Ethics Audit 

Crucially, this is not about self-shaming, finding out what you did “wrong,” etc. Ethical issues are rarely that straightforward. Pull out a scratch piece of paper and answer the following questions (1-2 sentences each):

- Did any ethical concerns come up during your work on your project? 
- Did you respond to these ethical concerns? 
- How did you respond? 
- How do you feel about your response?
- Are there things you would do differently/wish you could have done differently? 
- What were your constraints—time? (Money? Knowledge about their needs? Knowledge about the possibilities of technology? Support? Awareness? Etc.)
 
Return to thinking about communities served and/or impacted by your project. Imagine future extensions of your project that might more directly reach to communities you wish to serve.
