Borrowing from the discussion in the previous section, the digital health humanities is a conversation within the broader discourses in the digital humanities (DH). Just as DH is a relatively flexible and open umbrella under which many different projects fall, so too does the digital health humanities. Kirsten Ostherr describes the digital health humanities as “an emerging research method that blends critical analysis with the use of computational tools to explore research questions related to digital information and communication technologies (ICTs) in healthcare.”[^1] Health data—be it historical ‘archives as data’ information or the metrics that inform contemporary healthcare—can be examined from a humanistic perspective.

> The goal of the digital health humanities can be as broad as DH itself. Many of the scholars we worked as part of the Advancing Digital Health Humanities Institute hailed from disparate disciplines, from classicists and historians, to gender studies scholars and sociologists, but the linkage between scholarly object—health broadly—and digital methods enabled particular overlaps and caused generative frictions.

Extending Ostherr’s work we advocate for a digital health humanities that embraces interdisciplinary inspection as a means to address limitations and problems within DH, the health humanities, and other humanistic conversations. We see the digital health humanities as a space to navigate extant issues within healthcare as well as the **epistemic** foundations of computational and humanistic research.

We see the digital health humanities as being a space to address a few broad research categories:

**To Advocate and Support Social Determinants of Health Research**
- Provide Data and Frameworks to Link Historical Practice to Contemporary Inequities
- Critique Health Data Infrastructures (both present and past)
- Enable Collaborative and Interdisciplinary Research Programs

**To Critique Universal Humanism in Health Data Infrastructures**
- Building on Kim Gallon’s Black Digital Humanities, understanding Health Data as Assuming that some Communities did not Qualify as Human in its Epistemic Framing[^2]
- Engaging with the Entangled Health Humanities, seeing that Humanism ignores the natural[^3]

**To Skeptically Engage with the Production of Data At Scale, with Care Toward Private, Sensitive Health Data**
- To Produce Archives-As-Data Data Sets from Archival Health Information
- To Reconsider Data Infrastructures with Vulnerable Populations, Protected Communities, and Privacy In Mind (with HIPPA & NAGPRA always in focus)

## Footnotes
