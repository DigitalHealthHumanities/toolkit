Building ethical reflection and practices into your Digital Health Humanities project isn’t a legal requirement, and may not be valued highly by your funders or employers. However, as is often the case in emerging fields, moving too quickly without enough oversight and reflection can get people hurt. These could be the same people that you are making your project to support; and can damage the credibility of your own work, in the process. In other words, it’s a good idea to pay attention to ethics in the design of implementation of your Digital Health Humanities project. 

Incorporating ethics is not a one-off task. Ideally, it is a process that you engage in at the start of your work as you develop your research plan and the shape your project will take. Periodically during your project you can check in with the ethical commitments and questions established at the start; and at the end of your project, you can perform an “ethics audit” on your work, as part of a broader reflection on your project. This can point the way for further developments of your project, or for approaches that you want to share or to implement in your next project.

The purpose of this lesson is to introduce you to a set of tools that can be useful in exploring ethical questions. Some of these might be new or even feel uncomfortable, and I encourage you to be flexible and open to engaging with them. As with any tool–be it programming or theory–it’s ultimately up to you to decide whether or how it can support the work you want to do. 

>Considering ethics is not the same as satisfying legal or Institutional Review Board (IRB) requirements. A project can have IRB approval, but still lack care as to the ethics that arise specifically when dealing with–and especially when publicizing–digital health data. 

This lesson will introduce you to tools for exploring and addressing ethical considerations related to the following: 

1. Those whose stories are contained within your archive/data
2. The communities you hope your project will serve
3. Your own positionality—in relation to the above
