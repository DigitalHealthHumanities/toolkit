In this next step, we will engage with a primary source, which we will use in subsequent activities. Before we begin, let’s discuss what a primary source is. 

A {% include term.html term="primary source" %} can be defined as “an article, document, diary, manuscript, object, or information written or created at the time an event actually took place. Primary sources serve as an original source of information,” ([Library of Congress](https://ask.loc.gov/faq/303148)). Primary sources can also include oral histories or interviews about events that a person is retelling. Additionally, the transcript of the oral history or interview is considered a primary source.
 
Secondary sources typically provide a summary or analysis of a primary source. These types of sources can include textbooks, journal articles, and books, etc. 

{% include definition.html term="Primary Source" definition="A primary source refers to an object--document, photograph, testimony--that was created at the time an event occurred. Primary sources are the foundation of the practice of history because they can relay something about the past without a secondary mediator." %} 

{% include definition.html term="Secondary Source" definition="A secondary source is an object--document, graph, oral discussion--that was created after an event occurred but describes that event in question. In academic arguments, secondary sources often are the people we use to help support our reading of a primary source or our understanding of a past event, but we often do not rely on them entirely for our claims." %} 

The following is an example of a primary source. It is a transcript of an oral history that is part of the University of Texas at San Antonio Special Collections. The oral history was recorded in 1976 as part of the Institute of Texan Cultures Oral History Program, which was created by the Texas Historical Commission to document life in Texas and the history of San Antonio. You can find more information about the collection [here](https://txarchives.org/utsa/finding_aids/00317.xml).
