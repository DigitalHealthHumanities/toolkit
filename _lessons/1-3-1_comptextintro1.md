At its most basic level, text analysis involves counting words and analyzing their frequency. Text analysis typically works best with large datasets, which is why texts are often compiled around one million words. A collection of machine-readable texts is known as a {% include term.html term="corpus" %}. Multiple collections are referred to as {% include term.html term="corpora" %}.

{% include definition.html term="Corpus" definition="A corpus is a collection of written materials that have been made machine readable, and which can be analyzed in bulk using different computational text analysis methods." %}

For more accurate insights, especially when looking for patterns across genres or languages, having hundreds of thousands to a million words and more is often recommended (Large Language Models or LLMs).  When choosing texts for a corpus, it is important to consider what to include or exclude, and why. 

Here are some examples of corpora:

- All works by a specific **author**; all works by a medical humanist, for example, Rita Charon’s books or articles
- All works on a topic by **more than one author**, in a collaborative capacity or not; for example, the Mary Lasker papers at Columbia University, preserving correspondence, reports, bulletins, clippings, photographs, awards, and printed material from 1940 to 1993
- All works within a particular **genre** or **subgenre**; for example, medical records of a clinic
-  All mentions of a specific **concept** (e.g., a corpus of #MedHum tweets).

While counting is apparently straightforward, deciding what to count and why is much more challenging. In text analysis, there are two main approaches: 
    
1. Identifying **specific words or phrases** to study and looking for patterns 
2. Automating **pattern** detection, allowing the computer to identify relationships and present findings. 

Each method has its advantages and disadvantages, and the most interesting research often combines both approaches. Using machine learning and unsupervised methods provides a broad overview of language use across large corpora, offering fresh perspectives for **close reading**. Text analysis is an **iterative process**. Ultimately, text analysis is most effective when the detail you seek exceeds what you can manually analyze as a traditional reader, allowing you to explain the patterns you uncover.
